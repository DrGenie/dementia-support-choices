Accessibility

We want everyone to be able to use this website, including people living with dementia, older carers and people using assistive technology.

How the site is designed

  • Text uses Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for readers with low vision, at a large default size. You can make text larger with the text size buttons at the top of every page, or with your browser zoom.
  • Pages use plain language, short sections and the same navigation in the same place on every page.
  • Colours meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text. Charts use line patterns and labels as well as colour, and every chart has the same information in a table or text nearby.
  • All tools can be used with a keyboard. A visible yellow outline shows where you are on the page.
  • When you use a button or change a setting, a short message appears at the bottom right of the screen and is read out by screen readers. Messages use a traffic light with a word label: Good (green), Check (amber), Caution (red) and Done (blue). They stay while you hover over or focus them, can be closed with the X button, and otherwise disappear after about nine seconds. Nothing in them is lost: the same results stay on the page.
  • Small (i) buttons next to results open a plain-language explanation of the term and what it means for the programme on the page. They open with a click, Enter or Space, close with Escape or the X button, and return focus to the button. On small screens they open at the bottom of the screen. The same explanations are on the glossary page.
  • Longer sections are folded to keep pages short. Each has a heading with Show or Hide; they open by keyboard, open automatically when a link points into them, and all open when you print.
  • There are no time limits, and nothing moves on its own except these messages.
  • Results from the tools can be printed or saved as a PDF.

Known limitations

  • The interactive charts are drawn as images for screen readers, with a text summary next to each. The values can also be read by moving the pointer over the chart.
  • The calculators need JavaScript. The written information on each page can be read without it.

Report a problem

If something on this site does not work for you, please email mesfin.genie@newcastle.edu.au and tell us the page and what happened. We aim to reply within ten working days.

About this website

Dementia Support Choices presents the results of a discrete choice experiment with 264 Australian adults, carried out by researchers at the University of Newcastle. It gives information from research. It is not medical advice and does not replace advice from health professionals, aged care assessors or service providers.
