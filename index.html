Dementia support that people will choose to use

We asked 264 Australians to choose between dementia support programmes for a loved one. This website turns their answers into practical help for families, for the people who design programmes, and for those who fund them.

  • For families and carersIf you are looking for support for someone living with dementiaLearn about the main types of programme, work out what matters most to you, and get a list of questions to ask a provider.
  • Plan a programmeFor service providers and programme designersChoose the features of a programme and see how many people would be expected to take it up, and which change would help most.
  • Benefits and costsFor funders, policy makers and providersCompare the value of a programme to the people who use it with what it costs to run, and see whether it passes a benefit-cost test.
  • Fees and accessFor policy makers and fundersTest how a fee changes take-up, where people go when a fee rises, the effect of travel distance, and the cost of reaching more people.

What the study found

Include the carer

Programmes designed to support both the person with dementia and their carer were among the features people valued most.

Keep it close to home

Travel of 50 km or more was the feature people most wanted to avoid.

Weekly contact

Meeting weekly was preferred to meeting monthly, and daily contact was not preferred to weekly.

Counselling and community groups

Psychological counselling and community engagement were preferred to peer support. Virtual reality programmes were not.

The first dollars of a fee matter most

Moving from free to a small fee reduced the appeal of a programme about as much as moving from A$20 to A$50 per session.

Design will not reach everyone

About one in five people would not choose a programme even if it were free, whatever its features.

About the study

In an online survey in March and April 2025, adults from every Australian state and territory each compared eight pairs of support programmes. The programmes differed in type, whether they supported the carer, how they were delivered, how often and for how long people met, how far they had to travel, and the cost per session. After each choice, people said whether they would still take the chosen programme if they could choose neither.

From these choices we estimated how much each feature matters and how many people would take up a given programme. Read about the research, or look up any term in the glossary.

Using these results well

The numbers on this site describe what people said they would choose in a survey. They are a guide to what people value, not a forecast of how many will attend a particular service.

If you need support now, contact the National Dementia Helpline on 1800 100 500 or see where to get help.

About this website

Dementia Support Choices presents the results of a discrete choice experiment with 264 Australian adults, carried out by researchers at the University of Newcastle. It gives information from research. It is not medical advice and does not replace advice from health professionals, aged care assessors or service providers.
