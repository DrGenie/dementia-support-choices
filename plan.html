    How these numbers are calculated

    Take-up

    Take-up is the predicted share of people who would choose this programme rather than no programme if it were offered on its own. The main figure comes from a mixed logit model, which allows people to differ in how much they value each feature and in how willing they are to use any programme at all. The range is a 95% interval that reflects uncertainty in the estimates. A simpler model that treats everyone as having the same preferences is shown for comparison.

    Keeping the programme once chosen

    After choosing between two programmes, people in the study said whether they would keep their choice if they could choose neither. A separate model of that second answer gives the share who would keep this programme once they had picked it.

    Lived experience

    People in daily or weekly contact with someone living with dementia valued carer inclusion about three times as much as people without lived experience. The comparison uses a model that lets the value of carer inclusion depend on caring responsibilities, a close family member with dementia, and frequency of contact.

    Limits of these predictions

    These are predictions of stated choices from 264 Australian adults recruited through an online panel. They are not forecasts of attendance at a particular service, and they do not include provider costs, waiting lists or local availability. Costs outside A$0 to A$50 per session were not tested and cannot be selected.

    About this website

    Dementia Support Choices presents the results of a discrete choice experiment with 264 Australian adults, carried out by researchers at the University of Newcastle. It gives information from research. It is not medical advice and does not replace advice from health professionals, aged care assessors or service providers.
