How the numbers are calculated

Benefit to participants

For each person offered the programme, the mixed logit choice model gives the money value of having the programme available compared with no programme, net of the fee. Economists call this consumer surplus; it is calculated from all the choice probabilities at once (the "logsum"). It is measured per session, because fees and values in the study were per session. The yearly benefit is this value multiplied by the number of people offered and the number of sessions in a year, plus the fees participants pay.

Costs, fees and net benefit

The running cost is the provider cost per participant per session multiplied by the expected number of participants and sessions. Fees are paid by participants to the provider, so they appear on both sides and cancel out of the net benefit to society. The benefit-cost ratio is the total benefit to participants, including fees, divided by the running cost.

Traffic lights

Green: the whole 95% interval for the benefit-cost ratio is above 1. Amber: the interval includes 1, so the result is uncertain. Red: the whole interval is below 1.

What is not included

The benefit is what respondents valued about the programme when choosing for a loved one. Health effects people were not aware of, effects on carers' paid work, and savings to other services are not counted. The analysis covers one year, assumes participants attend every session, and assumes everyone offered the programme makes one decision about it. Values stated in a survey can be higher than what people would pay in practice .

About this website

Dementia Support Choices presents the results of a discrete choice experiment with 264 Australian adults, carried out by researchers at the University of Newcastle. It gives information from research. It is not medical advice and does not replace advice from health professionals, aged care assessors or service providers.
